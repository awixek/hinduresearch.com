Editorial & Research Policy

Last updated: 11 August 2026

This page explains how Hindu Research Portal approaches research, sourcing, evidence, and corrections for its historical, mathematical, scientific, philosophical, and cultural content. It is meant to help readers understand how a claim on this site is supported — not just what the claim says.

1. Our Research & Editorial Approach

Hindu Research Portal covers a wide range of material — from ancient scripture and inscriptions to Vedic mathematics, astronomy, philosophy, and modern research connected to Indian history and culture. Because these fields mix very different kinds of evidence, we try to write in a way that is evidence-aware rather than treating every claim as equally certain. In practice, we try to distinguish between:

  • Primary historical/textual evidence — original texts, inscriptions, manuscripts, and archaeological findings.
  • Scholarly interpretation — how historians, linguists, and researchers read, date, and contextualize that evidence.
  • Traditional accounts — knowledge passed down through scripture, oral tradition, or community memory, presented as tradition.
  • Modern scientific/academic research — findings from contemporary peer-reviewed or institutional research, where relevant to a topic.
  • Modern popular claims — statements that circulate widely (online, in books, or in conversation) but may go beyond what the underlying evidence actually supports.
Hindu Research Portal is an independent educational project. We are not a university, an academic journal, or a peer-reviewed research institution, and we do not claim that status. Our aim is to be a clear, honest starting point for learning — not a final academic authority.

2. Source Selection

Historical & Cultural Topics

Where possible, we try to prioritize sources roughly in this order, while treating this as general guidance rather than a rigid formula:

  1. Primary texts, reliable critical editions, and scholarly translations
  2. Scholarly books and academic research on the subject
  3. University or other institutional resources (archives, museum collections, academic departments)
  4. Reputable general references and encyclopaedic sources

Scientific & Medical Topics

  1. Peer-reviewed research, systematic reviews, and meta-analyses, where relevant to the specific claim
  2. Government and institutional sources (health authorities, research bodies)
  3. Reputable general references

This hierarchy is a guide to where we look first — it is not, by itself, proof that any individual claim is correct. Each claim still needs to be checked against what its specific sources actually say.

3. Evidence Classification

Where useful, articles may label a specific claim using one of the markers below. These labels describe how well-supported that particular claim is — they are not a judgment on whether a tradition, belief, or practice is "good" or "bad."

LabelMeaning
🟢 Well EstablishedStrong, consistent support for this specific claim from primary evidence and/or scholarly consensus.
🟡 Debated / UncertainSome evidence exists, but interpretation, dating, attribution, or conclusions remain genuinely disputed or limited among researchers.
🔴 Unsupported / Popular ClaimA widely repeated claim for which reliable supporting evidence is currently insufficient.

4. Our Method: Claim → Evidence → Interpretation → Caveat

For substantive claims, we try to follow a simple structure so readers can see the reasoning, not just the conclusion:

1. ClaimState clearly what is being asserted.
2. EvidenceNote what primary evidence or research actually exists for it.
3. InterpretationExplain how that evidence is generally understood or debated by scholars.
4. CaveatFlag uncertainty, disagreement, or limits of current evidence, where relevant.

For example, an article discussing an ancient dating question might state the traditional date, note what textual or archaeological evidence bears on it, summarize the range of scholarly views, and flag that the exact date remains debated — rather than presenting one figure as definitively settled.

5. Historical & Cultural Context

We try to present traditional accounts respectfully, as tradition, while being clear about where they diverge from what surviving historical evidence can independently confirm. Our goal is neither to dismiss tradition nor to present it as automatically equivalent to modern historical or scientific consensus — both would be misleading in different ways.

6. Scientific & Medical Content

Content that touches on Ayurveda, yoga, or other health-related traditional practices is presented for educational and research purposes. We try to distinguish clearly between what is a historical or traditional claim and what is supported by modern clinical evidence. This policy page does not itself make any medical efficacy claims, and content elsewhere on the site should not be treated as medical advice — see our About page and site disclaimers for more.

7. AI-Assisted Content

Some content on Hindu Research Portal, including tools like Rishi AI, Ayurveda AI, and Ramanujan AI, is produced with the assistance of AI tools for drafting, organizing, research workflows, or language tasks. AI output is a starting point or aid — it is not itself primary evidence, and it does not replace the source-checking described above. Rishi AI and Ramanujan AI are educational simulations, not the historical people they reference; their generated responses are not verified historical thoughts, statements, or quotations.

  • We do not present AI-generated historical-person responses as verified quotations, private thoughts, or authentic historical responses.
  • We do not claim that every sentence on the site has been individually, manually verified, because that would not be an accurate description of our current process.
  • Where AI assistance is used for a specific feature, we aim to be transparent about that rather than presenting AI output as if it were independently sourced scholarship.

8. Corrections Policy

We welcome credible corrections to historical, mathematical, scientific, and cultural information published on this website. When a significant factual error is verified, we correct the relevant content and, where appropriate, update the article's revision date.

Corrections are reviewed against credible evidence rather than accepted automatically. Not every submission is necessarily accepted — for example, if a suggested correction is itself unsupported, or reflects a genuinely disputed interpretation rather than a clear error. Where a correction is verified, we may update wording, sources, interpretation, and/or the article's last-updated date, depending on what the correction affects.

9. How to Submit a Correction

To submit a correction, please visit our Contact page and include:

  • The page URL
  • The claim in question
  • A reliable source supporting the correction
  • A brief explanation

10. Article Updates

Where applicable, article pages may display author/editorial attribution, a publication date, a last-updated date, and a sources/further-reading list. We do not commit to a fixed review schedule for every article; updates happen as errors are identified, new information becomes available, or a section is revised.

It is not a university, government institution, medical authority, or peer-reviewed journal, and nothing on this page or elsewhere on the site should be read as claiming that status.
