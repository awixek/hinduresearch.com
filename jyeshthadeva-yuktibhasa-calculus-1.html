Yuktibhasa: A Book of Rationale

Yuktibhasa is Jyeshthadeva's best-known work. MacTutor describes it as a famous Malayalam text and one of the main astronomical and mathematical texts produced by the Kerala school. It is unusual in an Indian mathematical context because it gives proofs of theorems and derivations of rules rather than only presenting finished formulas.

The work is traditionally understood as a consolidation and explanation of results developed by earlier Kerala scholars, especially Nilakantha's Tantrasangraha. Modern descriptions also characterize it as a treatise combining mathematics and astronomy.

Proofs, Derivations and “Yukti”

The word yukti is closely associated with reasoning, rationale or a method of establishing a result. In Yuktibhasa, the important historical feature is the explicit movement from a computational rule to an explanation of why that rule works.

Rule → Geometric / Mathematical reasoning → Derivation → Computation
A simplified representation of the explanatory style for which Yuktibhasa is known

This makes the text valuable to historians of mathematics because it preserves not only results but part of the reasoning culture surrounding those results. MacTutor specifically notes that the work gives proofs and derivations, an unusual feature among Indian mathematical texts of its period.

Infinite Series and the Kerala Mathematical Tradition

Yuktibhasa is especially important in the history of Kerala mathematics because it gives demonstrations associated with the series tradition for trigonometric functions. Modern scholarship identifies the Kerala school with results for sine, cosine and inverse tangent series, while Yuktibhasa provides detailed proofs and explanations for such results.

π / 4 = 1 − 1/3 + 1/5 − 1/7 + ⋯
The alternating series associated with the Kerala tradition's work on π

It is important to distinguish the historical fact from later popular labels. Yuktibhasa is exceptionally important for its mathematical demonstrations, but calling it simply “the world's first calculus textbook” compresses a complex historical development into a modern label. The safer formulation is that it contains important calculus-like ideas, infinite series and demonstrations of mathematical procedures.

Historical caution: The Kerala series tradition predates the European calculus tradition by centuries, but modern historians debate how best to map those techniques onto the later formal concept of calculus.

Drk-karana and Astronomical Observation

Drk-karana is another work associated with Jyeshthadeva and concerns astronomical observation and computation. Historical listings identify it alongside Yuktibhasa in his corpus.

The broader Kerala tradition placed value on improving computational astronomy through observation. This is consistent with the intellectual environment in which Jyeshthadeva worked: mathematical rules were connected to celestial phenomena and practical prediction rather than being isolated exercises.

Why this matters: Yuktibhasa represents the explanatory side of the tradition, while Drk-karana points toward the observational and computational side. Together they show how mathematics and astronomy functioned as a connected research program.
